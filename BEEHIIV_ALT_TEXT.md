## The Smallest Institution in the Campaign  (/p/the-smallest-institution-in-the-campaign)

**post23_fig1** — 16 CFR 255.5
> Printed text of 16 CFR 255.5, "Disclosure of material connections", paragraph (a): when a connection between the endorser and the seller of the advertised product might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement, and that connection is not reasonably expected by the audience, it must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. Material connections can include a business, family, or personal relationship.

**post23_fig2** — Google Docs Help
> Google Docs help page, "Create a named version": four numbered steps — open a document, spreadsheet, presentation or video; click Last edit at the top right; choose a previous version to see who updated the file; then click More and "Name this version". Notes that up to 40 named versions are allowed per document and 15 per spreadsheet.

**post23_fig3** — Adobe / Frame.io Help
> Adobe help page, "Create a version": share a revised creative asset with stakeholders for further review using the Create version button. The review link remains the same while a new version is created, so stakeholders can refresh the Frame.io view to see changes and compare versions.

**post23_fig4** — 17 U.S.C. 201(c)
> Printed text of 17 U.S.C. 201(c), "Contributions to Collective Works": copyright in each separate contribution to a collective work is distinct from copyright in the collective work as a whole and vests initially in the author of the contribution. Absent an express transfer, the owner of copyright in the collective work is presumed to have acquired only the privilege of reproducing and distributing the contribution as part of that particular collective work, any revision of it, and any later collective work in the same series.

## The Asset's Afterlife  (/p/the-assets-afterlife)

**post24_fig1** — 17 U.S.C. 201(d)
> Printed text of 17 U.S.C. 201(d), "Transfer of Ownership": (1) ownership of a copyright may be transferred in whole or in part by any means of conveyance or by operation of law, and may be bequeathed by will or pass as personal property under intestate succession; (2) any of the exclusive rights, including any subdivision of the rights specified in section 106, may be transferred and owned separately, and the owner of a particular exclusive right is entitled, to the extent of that right, to all the protection and remedies accorded the copyright owner.

**post24_fig2** — 17 U.S.C. 101 definition
> Printed statutory definition: a "transfer of copyright ownership" is an assignment, mortgage, exclusive license, or any other conveyance, alienation, or hypothecation of a copyright or of any of the exclusive rights comprised in a copyright, whether or not limited in time or place of effect, but not including a nonexclusive license.

**post24_fig3** — FTC guidance slide
> FTC guidance slide headed "When to Disclose": disclose when you have any financial, employment, personal, or family relationship with a brand. Financial relationships are not limited to money — disclose the relationship if you got anything of value to mention a product. If a brand gives you free or discounted products or other perks and you then mention one of its products, disclose even if you were not asked to mention that product.

**post24_fig4** — YouTube Help
> YouTube Help page, "Add paid product placements, sponsorships and endorsements": creators may include paid placements, endorsements or sponsorships but must notify YouTube by selecting the paid promotion box in the video details. All paid promotions must follow Google Ads policies and YouTube's Community Guidelines, and creators and brands are responsible for understanding and complying with local legal obligations about when, how and to whom to disclose.

## The Person Inside the Asset  (/p/the-person-inside-the-asset)

**post25_fig1** — 17 U.S.C. 101 definition
> Printed statutory definition of a "work made for hire": (1) a work prepared by an employee within the scope of his or her employment; or (2) a work specially ordered or commissioned for use as a contribution to a collective work, as part of a motion picture or other audiovisual work, as a translation, a supplementary work, a compilation, an instructional text, a test, answer material for a test, or an atlas, if the parties expressly agree in a written instrument signed by them that the work shall be considered a work made for hire.

**post25_fig2** — 17 U.S.C. 204(a)
> Printed text of 17 U.S.C. 204, "Execution of transfers of copyright ownership", paragraph (a): a transfer of copyright ownership, other than by operation of law, is not valid unless an instrument of conveyance, or a note or memorandum of the transfer, is in writing and signed by the owner of the rights conveyed or such owner's duly authorized agent.

**post25_fig3** — Community for Creative Non-Violence v. Reid
> Page 731 of the United States Reports, syllabus in Community for Creative Non-Violence v. Reid. Held: to determine whether a work is a "work made for hire" within the section 101 definition, a court should first apply general common law of agency principles to decide whether the work was prepared by an employee or an independent contractor, and then apply either section 101(1) or 101(2). Congress is taken to have used "employee" and "employment" in their settled common-law sense.

**post25_fig4** — U.S. Copyright Office, AI report Part 1
> Page from the U.S. Copyright Office report Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 1, Digital Replicas, under the heading "Copyright Act": copyright protects the underlying photographs or audio or video recordings from which a digital replica might be constructed, and replicas produced by ingesting or altering copyrighted works — such as superimposing a face onto an audiovisual work or simulating a voice — may implicate the reproduction and derivative-work rights. Copyright does not, however, protect an individual's identity in itself, so a replica of their image or voice alone would not constitute copyright infringement.

## The $2,000 Video  (/p/the-2000-video)

**post26_fig1** — 17 U.S.C. 202
> Printed text of 17 U.S.C. 202, "Ownership of copyright as distinct from ownership of material object": ownership of a copyright, or of any exclusive right under it, is distinct from ownership of any material object in which the work is embodied. Transfer of the object, including the copy or phonorecord in which the work is first fixed, does not of itself convey any rights in the work; nor, absent agreement, does transfer of the copyright convey property rights in any material object.

**post26_fig2** — 17 U.S.C. 201(a)-(b)
> Printed text of 17 U.S.C. 201, "Ownership of copyright": (a) Initial ownership — copyright vests initially in the author or authors of the work, and the authors of a joint work are coowners. (b) Works made for hire — the employer or other person for whom the work was prepared is considered the author and owns all of the rights comprised in the copyright, unless the parties have expressly agreed otherwise in a written instrument signed by them.

**post26_fig3** — U.S. Copyright Office circular
> Excerpt from a U.S. Copyright Office circular. A highlighted note states that mere ownership of a copy or phonorecord that embodies a work does not give the owner of that copy ownership of the copyright in the work. Below, under "Transfer of Copyright Ownership", it explains that any or all of the copyright owner's exclusive rights, or parts of them, can be transferred; that the transfer generally must be in writing and signed by the owner of the rights conveyed or an authorized agent; and that transferring a right on a nonexclusive basis does not require a written agreement.

**post26_fig4** — Apple Media Services Terms
> Apple Media Services Terms and Conditions, section F, "Services and Content Usage Rules": use of the Services and Content must follow the Usage Rules and any other use is a material breach of the agreement, which Apple may monitor. Content may be used only for personal, noncommercial purposes except as otherwise specified, and Apple's delivery of Services or Content does not transfer any commercial or promotional use rights, nor constitute a grant or waiver of any rights of the copyright owners.
